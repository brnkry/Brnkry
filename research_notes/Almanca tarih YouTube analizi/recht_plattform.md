# Legal and platform rules for German-language WW2 history videos (YouTube, Facebook/Instagram), 2024-2026

Context: "Chronik der Macht" (72% DE, 7.7% AT, 3.2% CH audience). Two Shorts (Erich Hartmann, Heinrich Ehrler) became unavailable in Germany, apparently because of visible unblurred Nazi symbols. Informational notes, not legal advice.

Research limitation: in this session direct page fetching was blocked by the network proxy (gesetze-im-internet.de, support.google.com, dejure.org all refused). All findings below come from search-result snippets and summaries, not full-page reads. Exact statute wording and current YouTube help-page wording should be checked by hand before quoting.

## 1. What §86a / §86 Abs. 4 StGB allow (Germany), and Austria/Switzerland

### Takeaway
§86a StGB punishes any public, perceivable "use" of symbols of banned (incl. Nazi) organizations; an approving or propagandistic intent is NOT required. A history video is only protected if it falls under the Sozialadäquanzklausel (§86 Abs. 4, applied via §86a Abs. 3), meaning the use objectively serves civic education, science, teaching, or reporting on history, and courts require that this purpose be recognizable, not only intended. In practice this makes a sober, clearly educational history video defensible, but unblurred symbols in short, context-poor, hero-styled Shorts are exactly the gray zone where platforms and authorities act first.

### Cited Findings
- Current §86 Abs. 4: Abs. 1 and 2 do not apply if the act serves civic education (staatsbürgerliche Aufklärung), defence against unconstitutional efforts, art, science, research or teaching, reporting on events of current affairs or history, or similar purposes. The clause was restructured by the version of 14 Sept 2021 (in force 22 Sept 2021); before that it was Abs. 3, so older commentary cites "§86 Abs. 3". — [rewis.io §86 StGB](https://rewis.io/gesetze/stgb/p/stgb-86/); [lxgesetze.de](https://lxgesetze.de/stgb/86); [buzer.de](https://www.buzer.de/86_StGB.htm)
- "Kennzeichen" are expressly flags, badges, uniform parts, slogans and greetings; look-alikes that can be mistaken for the original are covered too (§86a Abs. 2). — [Brandenburg administrative rule (bravors)](https://bravors.brandenburg.de/de/verwaltungsvorschriften-221316); [FSM decision 014-2020](https://fsm.de/entscheidungen/014-2020)
- "Verwenden" means any use that makes the symbol visually or acoustically perceivable, including images on the internet (BGH, 19.08.2014, 3 StR 88/14). — [BayObLG 2023 via gesetze-bayern.de](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2023-N-35650?hl=true)
- §86a is an abstract endangerment offence; an approving or promoting use is not required for liability. — [Bundestag Wissenschaftlicher Dienst, "Kennzeichen verfassungswidriger Organisationen"](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/1118130/kennzeichen-verfassungswidriger-organisationen.pdf)
- A BGH decision of 20.08.2025 (3 StR 484/24; 3 StR 519/24) is reported to have reaffirmed that propagandistic intent does not matter, only the public use. (Only via a law-firm blog; not verified in the original.) — [IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/ns-zeit-muenzen-briefmarke-postmarke.html)
- Courts exclude uses that show opposition to the organization "obviously and unambiguously" (e.g. a crossed-out swastika). BayObLG requires the opposition be recognizable even to a fleeting viewer "auf Anhieb" (Facebook/Instagram cases). — [BayObLG 07.10.2022, 202 StRR 90/22](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2022-N-28616?hl=true); [BZKJ paper "Hakenkreuze in Filmen"](https://www.bzkj.de/resource/blob/130174/3487010270e47902e1ccf9d1406b2591/201802-hakenkreuze-in-filmen-data.pdf)
- For Sozialadäquanz, subjective intent alone is not enough; the purpose must be recognizable and objectively served. — [BayObLG 2023 (summary of search result)](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2023-N-35650?hl=true)
- The FSM (Freiwillige Selbstkontrolle Multimedia) held that adding YouTube/Google/Facebook logos over a swastika graphic did not remove it from §86a because the swastika remained clearly recognizable and the dominant element. Implication: partial covering is not enough; the symbol must become unrecognizable. — [FSM 014-2020](https://fsm.de/entscheidungen/014-2020)
- Examples given for permitted reporting on history include TV programs about the Nazi era. — [Konrad-Adenauer-Stiftung: Welche rechtsextremistischen Symbole sind strafbar](https://www.kas.de/de/web/extremismus/rechtsextremismus/welche-rechtsextremistischen-symbole-sind-strafbar)
- Games/USK analogy: since 2018 the USK checks Sozialadäquanz case by case; the change came from a changed view of the Oberste Landesjugendbehörden, not a court ruling. Shows that the clause is applied case by case to media. — [gameswelt](https://www.gameswelt.de/unterhaltungssoftware-selbstkontrolle-usk/news/usk-nazi-symbole-nicht-mehr-grundsaetzlich-verboten-289660); [winfuture](https://winfuture.de/news,104486.html)
- Austria: Abzeichengesetz 1960 bans publicly wearing, displaying, presenting or distributing badges/emblems/symbols of organizations banned under §1 Verbotsgesetz. §2 Abs. 1 says the bans do not apply in listed cases "wenn nicht das Ideengut einer verbotenen Organisation gutgeheißen oder propagiert wird" (list of cases cut off in source). §3 contains penalties (administrative fine), not exceptions. — [WKO Wien document](https://www.wko.at/wien/handel/altwarenhandel/wkw-ns-20250924.pdf); [Tirol Kinder- und Jugendanwaltschaft "Rechtsextremismus 2024"](https://www.tirol.gv.at/fileadmin/themen/gesellschaft-soziales/kinder-und-jugendanwaltschaft/downloads/broschueren/Rechtsextremismus_2024.pdf)
- Austria Verbotsgesetz §3h criminalizes publicly denying or grossly trivializing Nazi genocide even without intent of Wiederbetätigung. — [Parliament doc](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XXIV/I/2048/fname_277498.pdf)
- Switzerland: currently displaying Nazi symbols is only punishable when combined with promoting the ideology. The Federal Council adopted the message for a new special law banning Nazi symbols in public on 19 June 2026; parliament still has to decide. Internet and audiovisual media would count as public space; sanction is a fine (earlier draft: CHF 200 Ordnungsbusse). Exceptions for science, education, media and art are reported. — [admin.ch / BJ](https://www.bj.admin.ch/de/verbot-von-nazisymbolen); [Beobachter](https://www.beobachter.ch/gesellschaft/politik/nazi-wanderer-riskieren-bald-auch-in-der-schweiz-strafen-699054); [Jüdische Allgemeine](https://www.juedische-allgemeine.de/juedische-welt/schweiz-will-mit-neuem-gesetz-gegen-nazi-symbole-vorgehen)

### Inferences
- A serious history channel is probably not committing a crime by showing archival symbols in an educational frame, but the Sozialadäquanz defence depends on the objective, recognizable purpose. A 30-60 second Short about a highly decorated Luftwaffe ace with music, dramatic cuts and no critical framing is weaker on "erkennbarer Aufklärungszweck" than a long documentary.
- Because intent is irrelevant for the base offence, and platforms cannot assess Sozialadäquanz case by case at scale, the cheapest risk-free route for a German-audience channel is to make symbols unrecognizable (blur/pixelate fully, not partial overlays).
- Austria's exception (no approval/propagation of ideas) looks somewhat more lenient in wording than Germany's purpose list; Switzerland is currently the most lenient but may tighten in 2026-2027.

### Gaps
- Exact current text of §86a Abs. 3 and §2 Abzeichengesetz could not be read directly (fetch blocked). The complete §2 AbzG list of exempt cases (reportedly print works, images, film/stage works, exhibitions) is unconfirmed.
- Reported Austrian 2024 Abzeichengesetz amendment (higher fines, more symbols) not verified.
- No German court ruling found that specifically concerns a YouTube history/education channel; the BGH 2025 decision is known only through a blog.
- Status of the Swiss bill in parliament after June 2026 unknown.

## 2. How YouTube handles it: legal geo-blocks, NetzDG to DSA, Community Guidelines (EDSA), age restriction, ads

### Takeaway
There are two separate tracks. (a) Local-law track: after a notice (user, authority, trusted flagger, or lawyer) YouTube checks German law and, if it sees a §86a issue, blocks the video only for Germany ("not available on this country domain due to a legal complaint"); the video stays up elsewhere and usually without a Community Guidelines strike. (b) Global Community Guidelines track (violent extremism / hate speech), where WW2 documentaries are allowed under the EDSA exception if context is clear in the video itself. The two Shorts fit track (a). Since 25 Aug 2023 the NetzDG no longer applies to YouTube; the EU DSA governs notices and requires statements of reasons and an internal complaint system.

### Cited Findings
- Google: due to the primacy of the EU DSA, NetzDG is not applied to YouTube from 25 August 2023 onward (NetzDG transparency reports are therefore historical). — [Google Transparency Report NetzDG](https://transparencyreport.google.com/netzdg/youtube)
- YouTube states its Community Guidelines apply globally; when a restriction affects only certain territories it may result from a legal complaint, an administrative or judicial order, or an assessment based on local legislation. Under the DSA, trusted flaggers can trigger such territorial action, but the final decision formally rests with the platform. (2026 case: Finnish podcast blocked EU-wide.) — [European Conservative](https://europeanconservative.com/?p=76329)
- Media regulator practice: the Medienanstalt Hamburg/Schleswig-Holstein (MA HSH) reported a video with NS references to YouTube; YouTube deleted comments and later blocked the video for users in Germany ("Regionalsperre"). — [MA HSH Hingucker archive](https://www.ma-hsh.de/infothek/publikationen/hingucker/hingucker-archiv.html)
- A law firm reports that a video with a swastika online for years was blocked for Germany within hours after the firm wrote to YouTube directly (partisan source). — [HAINTZ legal](https://www.haintz-legal.de/blog/detail/staatsanwaltschaft-ignoriert-hakenkreuz-auf-youtube-ueber-jahre-hinweg-haintz-legal-veranlasst-geoblocking)
- LG Frankfurt (Jan 2024) fined YouTube EUR 10,000, holding that blocking only in the German country version does not sufficiently implement a ban, since users can change location in a few clicks (personality-rights case; appeal status unclear). — [LHR law firm](https://www.lhr-law.de/en/magazine-en/media-law-personal-rights/frankfurt-regional-court-imposes-fine-of-e10000-against-youtube/)
- Wording "This content is not available on this country domain due to a legal complaint from the government" is the standard notice for local-law/government blocks, distinct from copyright region blocks. — [Daily News Egypt](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2013/02/19/google-blocks-youtube-videos); [DutchNews](https://www.dutchnews.nl/?p=71825)
- Violent extremist policy: content intended to praise, promote or aid violent extremist organizations is not allowed; YouTube may make exceptions for educational, documentary, scientific or artistic (EDSA) content incl. public-interest content. — [YouTube Help 9229472](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9229472?hl=en)
- YouTube's own example: a WWII documentary showing Nazi leaders' speeches may be allowed if it provides historical context and does not aim to support views promoted by the Third Reich. Reviewers look at title, description and context in the audio/imagery; for hate-speech-type content the context must be in the video itself (imagery or audio), and viewers must see the creator is not promoting it. Graphic material with context may get age restriction or a warning screen. — [YouTube Official Blog, "A look at how we treat EDSA content"](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/look-how-we-treat-educational-documentary-scientific-and-artistic-content-youtube/)
- Dec 2025: YouTube clarified/expanded the published EDSA exception criteria in its guidelines. — [Open Terms Archive memo](https://opentermsarchive.org/en/memos/youtube-details-out-criteria-for-educational-artistic-and-public-interest-exceptions-to-violating-content/)
- Enforcement is inconsistent in both directions: 2019 removal of anti-Nazi 1938 documentary "Inside Nazi Germany"; history teachers had Hitler archive clips removed or warned. Conversely, researchers say glorifying content stays up mislabeled as educational. — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/youtube-bans-anti-nazi-documentary-from-1938-for-violat-1837436638); [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/youtubes-new-policy-blocks-history-teachers-from-uploading-hitler-archive-clips/); [OHPI](https://ohpi.org.au/for-educational-purposes-online-the-loophole-being-exploited-by-nazis-and-extremists-online/)
- Advertiser-friendly guidelines: context is very important; violent/sensitive content in news, educational or documentary setting needs that context; hateful content has a separate section; automated monetization decisions can be appealed for human review. — [YouTube Help 6162278](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)
- Third-party: graphic or sensitive content may get limited ads even in documentary context if not presented with enough sensitivity/context. — [subscribr checklist](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-ad-friendly-content-checklist)

### Inferences
- The Hartmann/Ehrler Shorts are almost certainly local legal removals for DE (track a), triggered by a notice or by YouTube's local-law review, not a global guideline removal. German-only blocks hurt this channel disproportionately (72% DE audience).
- The LG Frankfurt reasoning pushes YouTube toward stricter handling for Germany, not looser.
- Expect WW2 content to frequently get limited ads (yellow icon) regardless of blurring; blurring helps the legal track more than the ads track. Requesting human review is the normal remedy.
- Shorts are disadvantaged for EDSA: context must be in the video itself, and Shorts have little room and descriptions are barely seen.

### Gaps
- Could not read the current full text of YouTube's violent extremism, hate speech, and advertiser-friendly pages (fetch blocked); no official statement found that explicitly says "WW2 = limited ads".
- No public data on how many German legal removals concern history channels.

## 3. Which symbols are problematic, and best practice

### Takeaway
Treat as problematic in Germany: swastika (all variants, incl. mirrored), Nazi flags, Reichskriegsflagge 1935-45 (it contains a swastika), Reichsadler with swastika, SS double Sig runes, SS-Totenkopf, Hitler portrait, Hitler salute, Horst-Wessel-Lied, and Nazi organization badges. Generally not §86a: Iron Cross and Balkenkreuz without swastika, older Reichskriegsflaggen and Reich flags without swastika, Wehrmacht as such. Best practice: fully blur/pixelate (not partial overlay), put critical context in the video (voice/on-screen text), sober titles.

### Cited Findings
- Reichskriegsflagge 1935-45 contains the Iron Cross plus the swastika; showing it is punishable under §86a. Other Reichskriegsflaggen (with Iron Cross) or general Reich flags are not covered (Bremen may fine them as Ordnungswidrigkeit in public). — [Brandenburg Verwaltungsvorschrift](https://bravors.brandenburg.de/de/verwaltungsvorschriften-221316)
- The Wehrmacht is not treated as a banned organization in the sense of §86 Abs. 1 Nr. 4. — [Bundestag WD 7-121-12](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/667980/2c0a6b0049ce0875cdbb20f79a57f0d5/WD-7-121-12-pdf-data.pdf)
- Iron Cross of WW2 may only be worn without swastika (weak source; forum/FAQ). Balkenkreuz/Iron Cross without swastika not banned, but propagandistic use can still create problems (forum source, weak). — [finanzfrage.net](https://www.finanzfrage.net/g/frage/darf-man-balkenkreuz-und-eisernes-kreuz-als-hoheitsabzeichen-auf-sein-auto-malen)
- Niedersachsen LKA lists swastika (all variations) and Reichsadler with swastika as punishable; Saxony guide adds mirrored/altered swastikas. — [kleineanfragen.de Niedersachsen 17/1775](https://kleineanfragen.de/niedersachsen/17/1775-neonazistische-propaganda-mit-symbolen-codes-und-kleidung-was-tut-die-landesregierung.pdf); [publikationen.sachsen.de](https://publikationen.sachsen.de/bdb/artikel/25382/documents/57475)
- SS double Sig rune is punishable; single Sig rune only as symbol of specific banned groups; SS-Totenkopf punishable as SS emblem, though skull designs depend on concrete form. — [Rheims, Symbole rechte Szene](https://www.arild-hauge.com/PDF/Rheims_Symbole_Rechte_Szene.pdf); [publikationen.sachsen.de](https://publikationen.sachsen.de/bdb/artikel/25382/documents/57475)
- BZKJ lists Hitler portrait, SS runes, Hitler salute, Horst-Wessel-Lied among covered Kennzeichen. — [BZKJ "Hakenkreuze in Filmen"](https://www.bzkj.de/resource/blob/130174/3487010270e47902e1ccf9d1406b2591/201802-hakenkreuze-in-filmen-data.pdf)
- No official exhaustive list of banned symbols exists; liability is case by case. — [Konrad-Adenauer-Stiftung](https://www.kas.de/de/web/extremismus/rechtsextremismus/welche-rechtsextremistischen-symbole-sind-strafbar)
- Symbols still clearly recognizable despite overlays remain covered. — [FSM 014-2020](https://fsm.de/entscheidungen/014-2020)
- YouTube EDSA: context must be in title, description, and in the video audio/imagery; for hate-type content in the video itself. — [YouTube Blog EDSA](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/look-how-we-treat-educational-documentary-scientific-and-artistic-content-youtube/)

### Inferences
- Hartmann (JG 52) and Ehrler (JG 5) photos/footage typically show Luftwaffe uniform eagle (Hoheitsadler with swastika), Knight's Cross ribbons (WW2 Ritterkreuz has a swastika in the center), aircraft tail swastikas (Bf 109 tail Hakenkreuz) and possibly Hitler award ceremonies. These are the likely triggers; Balkenkreuz on fuselage/wings is fine.
- Practical checklist: blur every swastika incl. small ones (uniform breast eagle, cap eagle, Ritterkreuz center, tail fin, flags, armbands); blur SS runes/Totenkopf; avoid or blur Hitler portrait shots; keep Balkenkreuz/Iron Cross unblurred; add a spoken or on-screen critical/contextual line ("Nazi regime", "war of aggression", costs/crimes) in the Short itself; neutral titles (avoid "Legend", "Hero", "greatest" framing without critique); description with sources and an education statement.
- A description disclaimer alone does not cure a visible symbol for German law (BayObLG: recognizable "auf Anhieb"), but supports the EDSA review.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source on the WW2 Ritterkreuz/Luftwaffe Hoheitsadler specifically (inference from symbol design: both contain a swastika).
- Whether the Hitler image alone (without symbols) triggers YouTube DE blocks in practice is not documented.

## 4. Can a geo-blocked video be unblocked? Re-upload? Creator experiences

### Takeaway
No documented, reliable public path was found by which YouTube lifts a German legal geo-block after the original video is edited. Under the DSA, the creator should receive a statement of reasons and can use YouTube's internal complaint/appeal system (and, in the EU, an out-of-court dispute settlement body), arguing Sozialadäquanz. The pragmatic route used by creators is to upload a new, blurred version (new video ID) and set the old one to private or delete it. Evidence for this is mostly anecdotal or inference.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Community Guidelines say creators are notified of removals and can appeal if they disagree; timelines and scope of appealable decisions are not specified. — [ConductAtlas summary of YouTube guidelines](https://conductatlas.com/platform/youtube/youtube-community-guidelines/creator-appeals-process/)
- For legal/government-driven blocks, the decision formally rests with the platform after a notice; reasons can remain unknown to the creator. — [European Conservative](https://europeanconservative.com/?p=76329)
- Older country blocks were tied to localized domains (Pakistan). — [ARY News](https://arynews.tv/after-three-yeon-in-pakistan)
- No concrete experience reports by named German history YouTubers (e.g. MrWissen2go) about Nazi-symbol geo-blocks were found. — searches returned none.

### Inferences
- YouTube Studio editor (blur tool) edits do not reliably trigger a re-review of a legal block; the edited video keeps the same ID and block status unless YouTube re-reviews after an appeal. Not confirmed by sources.
- Re-uploading a clearly blurred version is legally the clean solution (the new video no longer shows a §86a symbol). Risk: duplicate content looks; mitigate by deleting/privating the old one. Legal removals generally do not cause Community Guidelines strikes (they are not global policy violations), so the old blocked videos are not a strike risk, but they remain visible to YPP reviewers outside DE.
- Since older videos also show unblurred symbols and 72% of viewers are in DE, proactively fixing (blurred re-upload or private) the older ones reduces the chance of more blocks, which could follow any single notice (MA HSH / trusted flagger / lawyer practice).

### Gaps
- No official YouTube help text found on "edit after local legal removal" or a counter-notification form for non-copyright legal removals.
- No data on success rates of DSA appeals or out-of-court dispute bodies (e.g. User Rights, Appeals Centre Europe) for history content.

## 5. YouTube Partner Program review and deleting/re-uploading

### Takeaway
YPP reviewers look at the channel as a whole (main theme, most-viewed and recent videos, metadata) against Community Guidelines and YPP policies. German legal blocks are not Community Guidelines violations, but glorifying-looking Nazi imagery could raise "hateful/violent extremist" or advertiser concerns, and the "inauthentic/reused content" rule (stock/archive footage with AI voice) is the more common YPP rejection reason for documentary channels.

### Cited Findings
- Reused content is cited as a top YPP rejection reason; public-domain footage and clearly automated narration can trigger it. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/ru/blog/post/types-youtube-channels-dont-get-monetized); [boomlive copy](https://bfxt9q3pgjlvd832showjc47.vps.boomlive.in/?p=969)
- July 2025: "repetitious content" renamed "inauthentic content", covering mass-produced/AI TTS over stock footage with no original input (single third-party blog). — [ytshark](https://ytshark.com/?p=713)
- Reapply after 30 days; after repeated rejections 90 days (sources conflict; check Studio date). — [TunePocket](https://www.tunepocket.com/?p=62424); [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/youtube-monetization-rejected-appeal-or-reapply/)
- An appeal looks at the channel as it currently stands; advice not to delete videos before appealing, but fixes belong to the reapplication path. — [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/youtube-monetization-rejected-appeal-or-reapply/)

### Inferences
- Before applying/reapplying to YPP: blur symbols in top videos, make critical historical context visible in the video, use neutral titles/thumbnails (no swastika thumbnails), add original analysis/narration to counter "inauthentic content".
- Deleting a few blocked Shorts is low-risk (no strike consequences known); mass deletion/re-upload of many videos is not penalized per se but loses views/history. Setting old ones to private is a reversible alternative.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube source found stating that geo-blocked videos count against YPP review.

## 6. Facebook / Instagram (Meta)

### Takeaway
Meta's Dangerous Organizations and Individuals (DOI) policy removes content that glorifies, supports or represents hateful ideologies, naming Nazism explicitly, but allows content that reports on, neutrally discusses or condemns them. Historical posts are allowed only if the neutral/critical framing is clear; enforcement is error-prone. German law (§86a) additionally applies via geo-restriction on Meta too (BayObLG cases concerned Facebook/Instagram posts).

### Cited Findings
- Meta removes content that glorifies, supports or represents ideologies that promote hate, "such as nazism and white supremacy"; allowance for content reporting on, neutrally discussing or condemning dangerous organizations and individuals. — [Meta Transparency Center, DOI policy](https://transparency.meta.com/en-gb/policies/community-standards/dangerous-individuals-organizations/)
- Oversight Board (18 Dec 2023) overturned Meta's removal of four posts quoting Goebbels, faulting Meta for not separating supportive from neutral/condemning speech; Board recommended clearer examples for neutral discussion and news reporting. — [Columbia Global Freedom of Expression](https://globalfreedomofexpression.columbia.edu/cases/oversight-board-case-of-goebbels-quote/)
- 2025 Oversight Board symbols cases: a Kolovrat post removed under DOI; a post omitting the Nazi appropriation of the Odal rune flagged; a neutral-context Odal rune post left up. Shows "context and absence of hateful references" decide. — [Oversight Board public comments page](https://www.oversightboard.com/pc/symbols-adopted-by-dangerous-organizations/); [Columbia GFoE](https://globalfreedomofexpression.columbia.edu/?p=162352)
- German courts apply §86a to Facebook/Instagram posts (opposition must be recognizable at first glance). — [BayObLG 202 StRR 90/22](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2022-N-28616?hl=true)

### Inferences
- Same blurred versions of Shorts should be used for Reels; captions should frame critically (no "hero/legend" glorification).

### Gaps
- Current Meta wording on historical/educational images of Nazi symbols specifically, and whether Meta geo-blocks in DE for §86a, could not be read directly.
