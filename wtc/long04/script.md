# Long 04: Why your car won't start in the cold (the 5 sounds)

Hedef uzunluk: M/L (yaklaşık 5,5 - 6 dk, ~960 kelime). Ses tek parça kaydedilebilir.

## ElevenLabs metni

It's six in the morning. It's freezing. You turn the key, and you hear this. Or maybe this. Or nothing at all. Most people hear these sounds and think they all mean the same thing. They don't. Each sound points to a different problem, and once you know them, your car basically tells you what's wrong. So let's learn the five sounds of a car that won't start, and what to do about each one. First, why does this happen so much in winter? Your battery makes electricity through a chemical reaction, and cold slows that reaction down. According to AAA, at thirty two degrees Fahrenheit a battery loses about thirty five percent of its strength. At zero degrees, it loses about sixty percent. And at the same time, the cold makes your engine oil thicker, so the engine is harder to turn. AAA says it can take up to twice as much power to start an engine at zero degrees. So in winter, your battery gets weaker exactly when the engine needs more from it. And here's the twist. Summer heat is what really wears batteries out. Winter just exposes the damage. Most car batteries last about three to five years, so if yours is older than that, the cold will find it. Sound number one. Rapid clicking. This is the most common one. When you turn the key, a part called the starter solenoid tries to connect the battery to the starter motor. The starter needs a huge amount of current. If the battery is too weak, the voltage collapses, the solenoid lets go, the voltage recovers, and it tries again. Over and over. That's the clicking. Your dashboard lights will often dim or flicker at the same time. This almost always means a weak or dead battery, or a loose or corroded battery connection. A jump start usually gets you going. Sound number two. A single click, and then nothing. The lights stay bright, so the battery seems fine, but the engine doesn't turn. This often points to the starter motor or the starter solenoid. It can also be a bad connection at the battery or the starter. A good test is a jump start. If the car starts with a jump, the battery was the problem. If it still just clicks once, the starter is the likely suspect. Sound number three. Silence. No click, no lights, nothing. That usually means the battery is completely dead, or a battery cable is loose or badly corroded. If your car has push button start and the dash lights do come on, but the car won't start, check your key fob. Cold weather can kill a weak fob battery, and many cars have a backup method, like holding the fob right against the start button. Your owner's manual will show you how. Sound number four. A slow, lazy crank. The engine turns, but it sounds tired, like it's barely making it. This is a battery that's running out of strength, combined with cold, thick oil. If the car does start, don't celebrate. This is a warning. Get the battery tested soon, because the next cold morning might be the one where it doesn't start at all. Sound number five. The engine cranks at normal speed, but it won't start. This one is actually good news for your battery. The battery is strong enough to crank, so the problem is somewhere else, usually fuel, spark, or the security system. If a security or key light is flashing on the dash, the car may not recognize your key. Don't crank for more than about ten seconds at a time, and give the starter a break between tries. If it keeps happening, it's time for a mechanic. Now, how do you jump start a car the right way? Turn both cars off. Connect the red clamp to the positive terminal of the dead battery. Then the other red clamp to the positive terminal of the good battery. Then the black clamp to the negative terminal of the good battery. And here's the step most people get wrong. The last black clamp does not go on the dead battery. It goes on a clean, unpainted metal part of the engine or the frame, away from the battery. Batteries can release hydrogen gas, and the last connection can spark. Start the good car, wait a few minutes, then start the dead one. Remove the cables in reverse order. And if the dead battery is cracked, swollen, or leaking, don't jump it. A dead battery can freeze in very cold weather, and a frozen battery can be dangerous. After a jump, drive for at least twenty to thirty minutes, so the alternator can recharge the battery. Then get it tested. Many auto parts stores test batteries for free, and they can check your starter and alternator too. If you have a multimeter, a healthy battery at rest reads about twelve point six volts. Around twelve point four, it's only about three quarters charged. And with the engine running, you should see roughly thirteen point seven to fourteen point seven volts. That means the alternator is charging. To avoid all of this, keep the battery terminals clean. White or blue green crust on the terminals blocks current. Short trips don't give the battery enough time to recharge, so take a longer drive now and then. And if your battery is three years old or more, get it tested before winter, not after it leaves you stranded. So remember the five sounds. Rapid clicking, weak battery. One click, often the starter. Silence, dead battery or a bad connection. Slow crank, a battery that's about to quit. Normal crank but no start, look beyond the battery. Which sound did your car make on its worst winter morning? ## Kaynak notları
- AAA (bölgesel açıklamalar): 32°F'de akü gücünün ~%35'i, 0°F'de ~%60'ı kaybolur; 0°F'de marş için iki kata kadar güç gerekir.
- Akü ömrü ortalama 3-5 yıl; sıcak aküyü yıpratır.
- Hızlı tıklama: solenoid yeterli akım alamayıp tekrar tekrar çeker ve bırakır (Les Schwab, Firestone).
- Tek tık: marş / solenoid veya bağlantı; takviyeyle ayırt edilir (Les Schwab).
- Normal marş ama çalışmama: yakıt, ateşleme, immobilizer (yanıp sönen güvenlik lambası).
- Takviye sırası ve son pensin boyasız metale bağlanması (Edmunds, NAPA); çatlak / şişmiş / donmuş aküye takviye yapılmaz.
- O'Reilly ve AutoZone ücretsiz akü testi (metinde marka adı yok).
- Voltaj: 12,6 V dolu, 12,4 V ~%75, çalışırken 13,7-14,7 V.
