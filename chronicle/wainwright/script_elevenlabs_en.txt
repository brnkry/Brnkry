On September 2, 1945, General Douglas MacArthur signed Japan's surrender. Then he turned and handed a pen to the thin general standing behind him. Three years earlier, that man had surrendered to Japan. His name was Jonathan Wainwright. In March 1942, MacArthur left the Philippines, and Wainwright took command of the men left behind. On the island fortress of Corregidor they held out under constant shelling until May 6. Then he surrendered to save the lives of his men. The Japanese took him to a radio station in Manila and made him read the surrender orders on air. He became the highest-ranking American prisoner of the war. For more than three years he was moved from camp to camp, as far as Manchuria, afraid that America would remember him as the general who gave up. In August 1945, Soviet troops freed him. Two weeks later he stood on the deck of the USS Missouri. Weeks after that, he received the Medal of Honor, the award MacArthur had blocked in 1942. The man who had been forced to surrender watched Japan do the same.
