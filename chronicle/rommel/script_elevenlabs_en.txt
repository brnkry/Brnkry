October 14, 1944. A dark car stops outside a house near Ulm. Two generals step out. An hour later, Field Marshal Erwin Rommel gets in with them, and soon he is dead. Germany is told his war wounds killed him. That was a lie. In 1917, a young lieutenant named Rommel took Monte Matajur and, by his own count, about 9,000 Italian prisoners. In 1940, his panzer division raced through France so fast that even his own commanders lost track of it. They called it the Ghost Division. In North Africa, the British press gave him a name that stuck forever: the Desert Fox. But at El Alamein, he retreated against orders. In 1944, he told his superiors the war was lost. When the July 20 plot failed, his name came up. He was offered a choice: a trial, or poison and a state funeral. He chose poison. Brilliant soldier, or a myth built by both sides?
