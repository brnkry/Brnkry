Hitler sent two generals to his most famous field marshal with a choice: stand trial, or take poison. His name was Erwin Rommel. In World War One, as a young lieutenant, he stormed Monte Matajur and, by his own count, captured about 9,000 Italian soldiers. In 1940, his tanks tore through France so fast that even German command lost track of him. They called it the Ghost Division. Then came Africa. With a small force, he drove the British all the way back to Egypt. They feared him so much that their commander ordered his officers to stop saying his name. The British press called him the Desert Fox. At fifty, he became the youngest field marshal in the German army. But in 1944 he told Hitler the war was lost, and when the July plot failed, his name came up. On October 14, he said goodbye to his wife and son, got into the car, and took the cyanide. Germany buried him as a hero.
