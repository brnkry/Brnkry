October 14, 1944. A dark car stops outside a house near Ulm. Two generals step out. An hour later, Field Marshal Erwin Rommel gets in with them. Minutes later, he is dead. Germany is told his war wounds killed him. It was a lie. In 1917, as a young lieutenant, Rommel took Monte Matajur and, by his own count, about 9,000 Italian prisoners. In 1940, his panzer division moved so fast through France that even German command lost track of it. They called it the Ghost Division. In North Africa, he chased the British back to Egypt. Their commander had to order his own officers to stop talking about him. The British press called him the Desert Fox. Then came El Alamein, and he retreated against orders. In 1944, after the July plot failed, his name came up. The offer: a trial with consequences for his family, or poison and a state funeral. He chose poison. Four days later, they buried him as a hero.
