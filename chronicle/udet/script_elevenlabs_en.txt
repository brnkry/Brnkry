This sound was no accident. One man pushed for it, and when he died, the state lied about how. His name was Ernst Udet. In the First World War he shot down sixty-two planes, more than any German pilot except the Red Baron. In America he watched dive bombers fall almost straight down onto their targets. With money from Hermann Göring, he bought two and brought them to Germany. Many officers called dive bombing too dangerous, but Udet won, and he became the great champion of the Junkers Ju 87, the Stuka. Small propellers sat on its landing gear. The rushing air spun them, and the faster the plane dived, the louder they screamed. They were called the Trumpets of Jericho. Over Poland and France, that scream spread panic before a single bomb fell. Udet rose to run the Luftwaffe's entire aircraft production, a job the former stunt pilot was never made for. As the losses mounted, Göring blamed him. On November seventeenth, nineteen forty-one, Ernst Udet shot himself. Officially, he had died testing a new weapon.
