His own superiors shelved his plan. It defeated France in six weeks. Erich von Manstein was born Fritz Erich von Lewinski in 1887 and took the name of the uncle who raised him. In 1939 he proposed sending the tanks through the Ardennes, a forest the French believed tanks could barely cross. The army high command put the plan aside and sent him off to command a corps. Then, at a breakfast in February 1940, he explained it to Hitler himself. In May, the panzers broke through the Ardennes and reached the Channel. In Crimea he took Sevastopol and became a field marshal. But in November 1941 he signed an order asking his soldiers to understand harsh measures against Jews, while death squads killed tens of thousands behind his lines. He failed to break through to Stalingrad, and in 1944 Hitler dismissed him. In 1949 a British court sentenced him to 18 years. He was free by 1953. Two years later he published his memoirs and called them Lost Victories. The crimes barely appeared in them.
