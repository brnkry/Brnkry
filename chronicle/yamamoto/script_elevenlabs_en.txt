On April 18, 1943, sixteen American fighters flew low over the Pacific to kill one man. They knew exactly where he would be, because they had decoded his messages. His name was Isoroku Yamamoto, the admiral who planned Pearl Harbor. And he never wanted that war. As a young officer at Tsushima, he lost two fingers to a Russian shell. Later he studied at Harvard and served in Washington. He had seen American factories and oil fields with his own eyes. He warned his government that he could run wild for six months, maybe a year, and after that he had no confidence at all. They went to war anyway, and he planned the strike on Pearl Harbor. Six months later, at Midway, he lost four carriers in a single battle. Then American codebreakers cracked his travel plans. Over Bougainville, P-38s found his bomber and shot it down. It was exactly one year after the Doolittle Raid on Tokyo.
