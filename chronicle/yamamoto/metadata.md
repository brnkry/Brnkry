## Fact check (every claim in the video and the texts below)
| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| 18 April 1943, P-38s from Guadalcanal, 16 flew the mission | 18 were planned; 2 dropped out, 16 continued. "Sixteen" is correct. |
| They knew where he would be because they decoded his messages | His inspection itinerary was intercepted on 14 April and decrypted (JN-25). |
| Lost two fingers at Tsushima | 1905, index and middle finger of the left hand. |
| Studied at Harvard, served in Washington | Harvard 1919 to 1921; naval attaché in Washington 1926 to 1928. |
| "Run wild for six months, maybe a year" | Said to Prime Minister Konoe in 1940. The wording varies between translations, so it is never written in quotation marks below. |
| Four carriers lost at Midway | June 1942: Akagi, Kaga, Soryu, Hiryu. |
| Famous for being on time | His punctuality is widely documented, and the intercept was planned around it (the P-38s arrived at about 9:34). |
| Bomber shot down over Bougainville into the jungle | A G4M "Betty" near Buin. Rex Barber is generally credited; Lanphier and Barber shared the official credit. |
| Avoid | The "sleeping giant" quote. There is no evidence he ever said it (it comes from the film Tora! Tora! Tora!). |

**Description** (the first two lines appear under the Short, so the hook comes first)
```
On April 18, 1943, sixteen American P-38s flew low over the Pacific to kill one man: Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto, the architect of Pearl Harbor.

He never wanted that war. He lost two fingers at Tsushima in 1905, studied at Harvard and served as naval attaché in Washington. He had seen American factories and oil fields with his own eyes, and he warned his government that he could run wild for six months, maybe a year, and after that he had no confidence at all. Six months after Pearl Harbor, Japan lost four carriers at Midway.

Then American codebreakers read his travel plans. Yamamoto was famous for being on time, and that morning he was. Over Bougainville, P-38s shot his bomber down into the jungle. The Americans called it Operation Vengeance.
```
