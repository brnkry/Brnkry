In September 1940, a Polish officer walked into a German street roundup in Warsaw on purpose. He wanted to be sent to Auschwitz. His name was Witold Pilecki, and his mission was to find out what was happening inside the camp and to build a resistance there. He became prisoner 4859. For two and a half years he organized secret cells among the prisoners and smuggled reports out to the Polish underground. His first reports reached the Polish government in London in 1941. In April 1943, he and two other prisoners slipped out through the camp bakery at night and escaped. A year later, he fought in the Warsaw Uprising. After the war he came home and gathered intelligence on Poland's new communist rulers. In 1947 they arrested him. After a show trial, he was shot in Warsaw's Mokotów prison on May 25, 1948. His grave has never been found. He walked into Auschwitz by choice and got out alive. He did not survive the country he came home to.
