A British bomber dropped a wooden box by parachute over occupied France in August 1941. It was meant for one prisoner, Douglas Bader. Ten years earlier, Bader had lost both legs in a crash, but he flew again on artificial legs. Earlier that August, his Spitfire was shot down over France. As he bailed out, his right artificial leg got stuck in the cockpit and was torn off. The German fighter ace Adolf Galland invited him to his airfield and let him sit in his own Messerschmitt. The Germans found the lost leg in the wreck and repaired it. Bader used it to escape. He climbed out of his hospital window on knotted bedsheets, but was caught the next day. Galland sent word to England: send one plane with a spare leg, and it will not be shot at. The British did not want a favor from the enemy. One of their bombers dropped the box during a raid. Inside was a new leg for Bader. With two legs again, he kept trying to escape, so often that the Germans threatened to take his legs away at night. In the end they locked him up in Colditz. And Adolf Galland, the enemy who had asked for that leg, remained his friend for more than forty years.
